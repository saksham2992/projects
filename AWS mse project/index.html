Ocean Ecosystems

Each habitat shelters different life. Lose one and the others feel it.

Coral reefs

Warm, shallow, sunlit water

Reefs cover a tiny fraction of the seafloor but support roughly a quarter of all marine species. Rising sea temperatures cause bleaching that can kill whole reefs.

Mangroves & seagrass

Coastlines and shallow bays

These nurseries shelter young fish, store large amounts of carbon and soften storm waves before they reach villages and cities.

The deep sea

Below 200 metres

The largest habitat on Earth is still mostly unexplored. Deep-sea mining and bottom trawling threaten species we have not yet described.

Endangered Marine Species

Conservation status follows the IUCN Red List. Filter by how close each species is to extinction.

Critically endangered

Hawksbill turtle

Lives on coral reefs and eats sponges. Hunted for its shell and losing nesting beaches to development.

Critically endangered

Great hammerhead

Caught for its fins and as bycatch. Slow to mature, so populations recover very slowly.

Endangered

Blue whale

The largest animal ever to live. Ship strikes, noise and entanglement in fishing gear are the main threats.

Endangered

Whale shark

A gentle filter feeder that can grow past 12 metres. Threatened by fishing and boat collisions.

Endangered

Giant oceanic manta ray

Targeted for its gill plates. Gives birth to a single pup at a time, so losses are hard to replace.

Vulnerable

Dugong

Grazes on seagrass meadows. Loses food and habitat as coastal seagrass disappears.

How We Protect the Ocean

Big problems require practical, scalable answers.

♻️

Cut plastic at the source

Millions of tonnes of plastic enter the sea every year. Refuse single-use items, join a beach clean-up and support bans on plastic bags.

🛡️

Create marine protected areas

Protected zones let fish stocks and reefs recover, and fish often spill over into nearby fishing grounds.

🐟

Choose sustainable seafood

Ask where your fish comes from and pick species caught with low-impact methods. Avoid shark fin and overfished species.
